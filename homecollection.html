02 How it works

A morning in the life
of your sample.

Book a 7 AM slot and this is the shape of the day. Times are typical for a routine blood test collected inside Bhubaneswar.

6:45 AM

You book

A call or this page's form. We confirm the test, the exact price and whether you need to fast — before anyone sets off.

7:05 AM

Your kit is packed

Tubes are chosen for your specific tests and labelled with your name and a barcode at the lab, not at your door.

7:30 AM

The doorbell rings

Photo ID first, then the kit is opened in front of you. The draw itself takes two to three minutes.

8:15 AM

Into the cold box

Sealed, logged and carried at 2–8 °C. Samples from inside the city reach the Jagamara bench within the hour.

9:00 AM

Controls, then you

Each analyser runs its quality controls first. Only when those read true does your sample go on.

1:30 PM

Report on your phone

Verified and released by our pathologist, then sent to WhatsApp and email. A printed copy waits at the centre.

    04 Who rings your doorbell

    A stranger in your
    home, done properly.

    Letting someone in to take blood asks a lot of trust. These are the things we do so you never have to take it on faith.

    • Photo ID before anything else

      Every collector carries a dated Kamini Clinic & Labs card with their photograph and staff number, and shows it at the door unprompted.

    • Trained hands, not a courier

      Sample collection is done by phlebotomists trained on venepuncture, tube order and patient identification — the same staff who work our lab counter.

    • The kit is opened in front of you

      Needles, tubes and swabs are sealed and single-use. The seal is broken where you can see it, gloves go on fresh, and nothing is reused between patients.

    • Sharps leave with us

      Used needles go straight into a puncture-proof container and travel back to the lab for disposal. Nothing medical is left in your bin.

    • Tell us who it is for

      Elderly, bedridden and needle-anxious patients are routine for us. Ask for a female collector when you book and we will arrange one wherever possible.

    What we bring

    Everything below travels from the Jagamara lab in a sealed carrier and comes back with us.

    • Pre-labelled tubes chosen for your exact tests, with your name and barcode already on them
    • A sealed single-use kit — needle, holder, swab, tourniquet and dressing
    • Fresh gloves and hand sanitiser, changed for every patient
    • A cold box at 2–8 °C so the sample is as good at the bench as it was at your table
    • A sharps container for safe disposal back at the lab
    • A sterile urine container when your tests need one

    Keep this ready

    Two minutes of preparation makes the visit quicker and the result cleaner.

    • A chair by good light with a flat surface beside it — a dining table is perfect
    • The prescription, if your doctor gave one, so we collect for the right tests
    • Previous reports for the same test, which help our pathologist read the trend
    • A list of medicines you take, including any you took that morning
    • Loose half-sleeves — easier on the arm than a tight cuff you have to fight
    • Something to eat afterwards, especially if you have been fasting since the night before

    Water is allowed

    Fasting means no food, tea, coffee, milk or juice. Plain water is fine and actually helps — a well-hydrated vein makes the draw quicker and easier.

    Don't stop your medicines

    Take your regular tablets as usual unless your doctor has told you otherwise, and tell the collector what you took that morning — it goes on the requisition.

    Book the morning for fasting tests

    An overnight fast that ends at 7 AM is comfortable. One that has to last until an afternoon slot rarely is — so we keep the 6–9 AM windows for fasting work.
